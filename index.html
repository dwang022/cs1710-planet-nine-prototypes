CS 1710 · Week 9 · Dashboard prototypes

Does the alignment survive, whichever objects you trust?

Two ways to wire the same three views together. Both use the real catalogue of 90 extreme objects — the same data as the main site.

Prototype A — Cross-filter

One selection, live. Drag a box on the scatter and the other two views recompute instantly.

Tests whether live filtering makes the sample-dependence obvious.

Prototype B — Two-sample comparison

Two selections side by side. Define A and B, see both results at once with the difference called out.

Tests whether direct comparison beats discovery-by-dragging.

The thing to look for: select the objects above the grey line (closest approach > 45 AU — the ones Neptune cannot reach) and tick "well-measured orbits only". Agreement rises to 0.611 across 11 objects, the strongest in the dataset, against 0.082 for everything. Either filter alone gives far less (0.360 and 0.332). Splitting by distance instead barely moves the needle at all. The open question is which of these two layouts makes that two-filter combination easier to stumble on.

Runs on port 5174, separately from the main site on 5173. Data: JPL Small-Body Database cross-checked against the Minor Planet Centre.
